# Print review A (independent, full-size faces)

Bar: Jurassic Park outside.png and inside.png, plus the "Hard quality gate" in BRIEF-PRINT.md. Builder self-grades ignored. Dash scan over every face html: clean in all 12 folders. No real brand names spotted.

None of the 12 pass. The common failure: most of them still look like a modern web or Canva card in a period colour scheme. The type is crisp, the colour is flat, they're too clean and evenly spaced, and nothing shows misregistration, halftone, letterpress bite or photocopy toner. The second common failure is food copy written in 2010s menu language ("whipped feta", "dry-brined", "flaky salt", "crème fraîche", "sweet chili") on objects dated 1977 to 1993.

## chud (8.8): soup kitchen wall notice, letter
Strongest of the set. Right object, good Easter eggs (Bosch phone note, George Cooper photo credit, "Where is everybody?"), and the manhole halftone works.
1. It doesn't look photocopied. The paper is clean cream and the type is laser-crisp. Add toner speckle, slightly grey and uneven blacks, a darker copier edge shadow, a hair of skew and a lost corner. Make it a 1984 church-basement Xerox, not a clean print.
2. The pushpin is a glossy CG sphere with a soft drop shadow, which is a modern UI element. Swap it for a flatter painted pin or masking tape, and add a pin hole with a small tear.
3. Food and copy fixes. "Lime cordial and tonic water" is British-ish wording, so use "lime juice and seltzer". "HOT MEALS" over "HOT COFFEE" repeats the word. "Most of you call me the Reverend" reads like a script line, not something he'd type on his own notice. Cut it or change it to "Ask for the Reverend."

## youve-got-mail (8.2): bookstore cafe card, 5.5 x 8.5 in
1. The steaming cup icon (front header and back title) is exactly the flat vector icon the gate bans. Remove it, or replace it with a period line-cut engraving.
2. Front and back both read as a 2020s Canva cafe card: Libre Franklin body, a tinted panel with a left accent bar on the back, a perfect coffee-ring PNG cut off at the top right. In 1998 Fox Books would have run a glossy four-colour chain piece with a house logo lock-up, a price grid and a legal line. Add store chain furniture (location list, store hours, the "Fox Books" family of stores) and print the facade still as a coated-stock four-colour image with some dot gain, not a clean photo.
3. Food copy. "Decaf cappuccino... a deep cap of foam" repeats "cap" and is odd as a headline item, so make it "Cappuccino" with "decaf on request". "Twice cooked" biscotti is fine, but "made for dunking" sits right beside the "Brewed fresh" coffee filler. Add one more substantial cafe item so the back's "Around the store" panel isn't carrying the page.

## moonstruck (8.1): Italian dinner card, letter
1. The Brooklyn Bridge skyline is a flat geometric vector with dot-string lights, and the bridge is drawn wrong (a single tower with asymmetric cables). It fails the gate outright. Repaint it as an engraving or a two-ink line drawing with real gothic twin arches, or use a TMDB still of the bridge and the moon as a navy duotone halftone.
2. The back has a dead band of roughly 10% of the page between the two columns and the "Dopo l'opera" box, and the moon "logo" is a flat ring icon. Close the gap: move the box up and add a short antipasti or grappa list. Drop the icon.
3. Food copy repeats itself: "chopped parsley" twice, garlic in four dishes, and "olive oil" twice on the front. Vary the finishing lines. Pinyon Script with EB Garamond also reads like a wedding invitation template. A 1987 Brooklyn place would more likely use a heavier engraved script and a condensed Bodoni.

## sleepless-in-seattle (7.7): diner placemat, legal landscape
1. The houseboat art is flat geometric vector (rectangles, flat roofs, line water). It fails the gate. Repaint it as a one or two-colour line cut, or a TMDB houseboat still screened in the teal ink.
2. There's a large dead zone in the middle column below the grilled cheese, over 10% of that column, and the kids' drawing box is empty. Placemats are dense: add puzzles, a Seattle trivia strip, a ferry schedule or a kids' maze, and fill the column. Add the grease-proof sheen and a stronger coffee ring that the brief asks for on a placemat.
3. The food wording is 2010s, not 1993: "crème fraîche", "flaky salt", and "brown butter" twice (popcorn and cookies). "Lemon" appears twice in the salmon description. Jost and Yellowtail read as modern retro. Use a period diner script and a 1990s sans.

## tootsie (8.3): pink TV rehearsal schedule, letter
1. The back is written in modern sans-serif web style (Archivo body, heavy red bars) with 2020s catering food: "roasted red pepper and feta dip with thick yogurt, chili flakes, seeded crackers" and "salted the night before" chicken. A 1982 studio lunch sheet would be typed on the same pink stock as the front. Retype the back in Courier and rewrite the food in 1982 words (chicken salad platter, cold cuts, cole slaw, roast chicken with pan gravy). "Cress" should be "watercress".
2. The front's heavy Archivo masthead and solid red table bars look like a web table. A production office in 1982 would have a typewritten or photocopied form with ruled boxes. Thin the bars and add carbon or photocopy texture.
3. The Julie Nichols role cell is blank, which reads as unfinished. Fill in her character name only if it's certain, or put "(as cast)". The Polaroid frame is the wrong proportion for a 1982 SX-70 or 600 print, so make it square with the thick bottom border.

## manhattan (8.3): caterer price folder, letter landscape
1. Period error: "Szechuan peppercorns". These were banned from US import from 1968 to 2005, so a 1979 New York caterer could not list them. "Whipped ricotta with lemon zest and honey" toasts, "thigh meat" and "Toasts, two ways" are 2010s menu language. Rewrite in 1979 caterer wording (canapés, rumaki, cocktail franks, a cold poached salmon). "Whipped" appears three times across the inside.
2. Jost light lowercase headers plus Libre Caslon look like a modern minimalist brand deck. A 1979 caterer folder would be Futura or Avant Garde on textured cover stock with letterpress or offset. Add visible impression and a slightly cooler ink.
3. Dead space at the bottom of the inside left panel under the waffles, about 10% of the panel. Fill it with a "suggested party for twelve" line or a sample order. "Kung pao" sent by a caterer in Chinese cartons is a stretch for the object. Frame it as "Chinese supper from our friends downtown" or similar.

## annie-hall (8.6): goldenrod deli takeaway menu, letter
1. There's no period print texture. The goldenrod stock is a flat fill, and the starbursts are perfect vector shapes. Add offset misregistration between the brown and red, rough ink edges, slight fill-in on the heavy Fraunces, and paper fibre on goldenrod. That's what makes it a 1977 deli menu and not a retro poster.
2. Repeated words: "Pastrami" in both sandwich names and "steamed" in both descriptions, "every hour" on both the knish and the coffee. The California cooler ("cucumber, lime, mint and honey") is not 1977. Make it orange juice and seltzer, or a lime rickey.
3. The back has loose spacing around the handwritten note, and the "We deliver" footer with a second starburst copies the front. Use that space for a second fountain or bake-case column (rugelach, black and white cookie) or a platter price list. That fills it the way real deli menus are packed.

## trading-places (8.3): gentlemen's club bill of fare, 8.5 x 14 in
1. It's the exact "cream card plus centred serif plus monogram seal" the brief tells you to avoid, and there's no art at all. Add a period engraving of the club's facade or reading room, or a TMDB still as a sepia halftone, and add letterpress bite to the type.
2. The orange butter cake is "brushed with frozen juice concentrate". No Philadelphia gentlemen's club would print that, and it clashes with "fresh zest". Keep the recipe and wording plausible ("orange syrup"), and let the steward's notice carry the OJ wink.
3. The back's right column is dead between the coffee and the Wager Book box, and the flat coffee ring sits in that hole. The paper-clip notice is good, but the clip is a flat vector. Fill the column with a wine list or a cigar line and repaint the clip.

## quick-change (7.5): airport lounge tall table card, 4.25 x 11 in
1. The header sunset, runway and jet silhouette are flat gradient vector. That's the clearest gate fail in the batch, and the swept-wing silhouette is generic, not a 1990 airliner. Replace it with a TMDB still of the airport or plane as a halftone, or a two-ink line drawing of a period JFK terminal.
2. The split-flap board uses a broken pixel font ("N" and "M" glyphs misdrawn) and a dark rounded panel like an app widget. The food is 2010s: "whipped feta", "sweet chili sauce", "cilantro" on wings, and the "Wheels Up bourbon smash" name. Rewrite for 1990: Buffalo wings with blue cheese, a club sandwich, nachos.
3. Hours contradict each other. The front says "Kitchen to 11PM, last call 11:30" and "Light fare from 11 AM", but the back says "Open daily 6 AM to midnight, last call 30 minutes before the final departure". Make them agree. There's also dead space above the back footer.

## brewsters-millions (8.6): Fifth Avenue hotel in-suite dining card, letter
1. The facade is painted, which is better than flat vector, but it reads as digital gouache: identical window blobs and a green tree of round stamps. Add engraving or a two-ink screen and vary the windows. The facade also reads closely as the real Plaza (green mansard roof, Fifth at the park), so change enough of the architecture to make it clearly the fictional Vandermeer.
2. The pink panel with notched corners and black tab labels looks like a web card. Real 1985 room-service cards used embossed or foil covers and plain inside pages. Lose the tabs and add foil or emboss texture to the gold.
3. The "Vote None of the Above" button is printed as part of the hotel card. Make it read clearly as a pin or sticker stuck on (shadow and edge), or drop it. "Whipped cream" is repeated across sweets and breakfast. "Crème fraîche" on the 1985 blini is fine, but "snipped chives" is modern menu speak.

## secret-of-my-success (8.5): interoffice memo, letter
1. The Pemrose logo is a flat bar-chart icon, and the staple is a grey pill. Both are modern vector. A 1987 corporate memo has an engraved or typeset wordmark and a real staple with shadow and two punch holes. Real memos were typed and photocopied, so print the whole thing in one ink with copier grey. Two-colour headers with dotted price leaders are over-designed for a memo.
2. Food copy: "Dry-brined, seared hard and basted in butter with thyme and garlic" is 2010s steakhouse language. "Spring onion" breaks the American spelling rule (should be scallions). "Crème fraîche" on blini should be "sour cream" in 1987. "Farmhouse apple pie" repeats Tootsie's "Farmhouse".
3. The front has dead space between the sole and "(continued)", over 10% of the face. Pull page 2's cafeteria intro up, or add a "This week's specials" box. The Archivo labels (TO, FROM) look like a modern form, so make them typed caps.

## working-girl (8.2): partners' dining room luncheon card with order slip, 4.25 x 11 in
1. Cream card, centred serif, PM monogram seal: the cliche the brief tells you to avoid, and there's no art or texture beyond a vignette. Add a period engraving (the Staten Island Ferry or the Manhattan skyline from the water, as a line cut) and letterpress bite.
2. The front checkmarks sit about 30px from the trim edge, which breaks the 40px rule. Move the ticks inside the margin. The back's perforation dots run right to the trim, so make a proper perforation with a scissors mark.
3. Food repeats: "mayonnaise" on both the shrimp and the crab cakes, "Chablis" twice on the bar list. A Manhattan with bourbon on a 1988 Wall Street card is fine, but rye is truer. Add one more luncheon plate (sole or a cold lobster plate) to fill the front lower half.

## Rule breaches found
- Dashes: none.
- Real brands: none printed. The Brewster's facade is close to the real Plaza; change the architecture.
- Alcohol in family films: none of these are family films. Quick Change has a kids' line on a card that also lists cocktails, which is fine for an airport lounge.
- Period error (hard fail on its own): Manhattan "Szechuan peppercorns" in 1979.
- Gate fails for flat vector art: Moonstruck skyline, Sleepless houseboats, Quick Change header, You've Got Mail cup icon, Secret of My Success logo and staple.
