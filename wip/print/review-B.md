# Print menu review B (independent, Oct 2026)

Judged against BRIEF-PRINT.md, mainly the "Hard quality gate". Reference bar: jurassic/outside.png and inside.png. I opened every face PNG. I ignored the builders' own grades. Dash check: no em or en dashes in any face file.

Nothing passes. Three problems come up again and again:
1. Flat vector art. Trains, boats, toy blocks and baseballs are drawn as clean geometric icons, which is what the gate bans.
2. Modern type. Several faces use variable Google grotesks set like a web page (Hanken, Barlow, Libre Franklin) with no sign of how they were printed: no impression, no misregistration, no ink spread.
3. British spellings in American films.

Yellowtail script appears on both Money Train and Inside Man, so those two read as the same kit.

## three-men-and-a-baby: 8.8, REDO (close)
Object: a 17x11 diazo whiteprint of the loft (sheet A-1) with a supper schedule and title block. This is the strongest of the batch. The object is specific, the title block gags work (Drawn Peter, Checked Michael, Approved Jack (away)), and the highlighted 2 A.M. / 6 A.M. bottle is a nice touch. The food is sound and reads right to a chef.

The problem is the drawing. It looks like a clean CAD export, not a 1987 hand drawing run through a diazo machine.

Fixes:
1. Make it a real 1987 whiteprint. Give the background the uneven blue-violet diazo tint with a faint ammonia streak. Vary line weight like ink pen on vellum (heavy walls, fine furniture). Add slight line bleed, and show the creases where a print gets folded down to fit a flat file.
2. Fill the plan. The living room and studio are nearly empty (one sofa, two boxes). Add Michael's drawing board, Peter's drafting table, a crib, a changing table and a stereo, with dimension strings and door swings. Fill the dead band in the title block under Revision 2 with a Revision 3, a stamp or a key plan.
3. Check "Johnny Cool is still due". I cannot confirm it is in the film, so cut it if it is not. Then fix the contradiction: note 2 says "nothing hot near the bassinet" but the plan puts the bassinet right next to the table. Move it, or make that the joke on purpose with a revision cloud.

## frequency: 8.2, REDO
Object: a firehouse World Series window card, Saturday Oct. 11, 1969 (that date is correct for Game 1). The eggs are good: Little Chief, Frank or Satch, Julia's pie. The Mets orange and blue is right. But it reads as a 2020s Canva poster: crisp Anton and Alfa Slab, flat colour blocks, and a vector baseball.

Fixes:
1. Print it like a 1969 letterpress or silkscreen window card. Use wood-type headline letters with uneven ink coverage, two spot inks slightly out of register and a heavier card stock. Swap the vector baseball for a line-cut or engraved ball, or a period halftone of the firehouse.
2. Period food wording. "Baked wonton shells" and "Granny Smiths" are unlikely on a 1969 Queens firehouse card. Keep the food but describe it as crisp baked pastry cups and tart green apples. Lemonade is "fresh-squeezed" in the shandy and "Squeezed this morning" again lower down, so cut one.
3. Fix the pricing. "20¢ / 1.69" is missing the dollar sign, so write "slice 20¢, whole pie $1.69". Replace the "See Frank or Satch" marker font (Rock Salt reads as modern) with a period ballpoint or grease-pencil hand.

## out-of-towners: 6.0, REDO
Object: a 1970 railroad dining car and lounge car card. The object is right, but the art is the exact thing the gate bans. The train is a flat vector modern high-speed set with a pantograph and a nose like a 2000s Acela, under a striped "retro sunset". A 1970 Boston to New York train would be Penn Central (ex New Haven) diesel or electric with heavyweight or streamlined stainless cars.

Fixes:
1. Repaint the cover art as a period engraving or two-colour line cut of a correct 1970 Northeast train (FL9 or EP-5 style locomotive, fluted stainless cars) along the Connecticut shore. Delete the sunset stripes.
2. Fill the faces. The front has only 4 dishes and a near-empty lower fifth. On the back, a big dead band runs from Tom Collins down to Your Route, and the right column is empty under the counter box (well over 10%). Real 1970 dining car menus are dense: add table d'hote breakfast, a sandwich board, a children's line, and a meal-check note with the "Write your order" pad lines.
3. Change the typography. Hanken Grotesk plus Source Serif in a web layout looks like a 2020s brand site. Use period railroad menu faces (a Bodoni or Century with condensed gothic heads), a printed rule border and a two-colour letterpress finish. Also, "Seared to your liking ... fried as you like" repeats "like".

## splash: 7.0, REDO
Object: a Cape Cod excursion boat handout plus galley menu, season of 1984. Good object, and "Most guests leave the shell" is a clever wink. But the art is flat vector (boat, lighthouse, striped sunset) with a mechanical dot screen laid over it, and the fonts are Fraunces and Libre Franklin set like a modern website.

Fixes:
1. Repaint the cover as a 1984 two-colour offset handout. Use a real photo-halftone of the harbour or boat (a TMDB still if it fits), or a pen-and-ink drawing with slight red/blue misregistration. Kill the striped sunset, which is also on Out-of-Towners.
2. Rule and spelling breaches. "sea-coloured" should be "sea-colored". Splash is a PG Touchstone family picture, so the vodka Blue Lagoon should come off or become an alcohol-free blue lemonade. Lager batter should be called a beer-free crisp batter or "cold soda batter" (same food, if the recipe allows). Don't say "fries" under a dish named "Fish and chips". Pick one word.
3. Add density and specifics. The back is fine but generic. Add a kids' line (the boat would carry one), a tide or sailing table, a harbour map in line art on the front in place of some of the fare text, and one more film wink (Cape Cod, 1964, the bell-shaped buoy).

## big: 6.5, REDO
Object: a MacMillan Toy Co. company party menu dressed as a toy box. Fun idea with good eggs (baby corn eaten like a cob, the caviar, "Building does not turn into a robot", the white tuxedo). But it is the most modern-looking piece in the batch: rounded-corner cards, a gradient-shaded vector block stack, a starburst, and Titan One. It reads as a 2020s web banner, not a 1988 box.

Fixes:
1. Rebuild it as a printed 1988 box face. Use a flat four-colour process look with a visible rosette screen, a die-cut window outline, a product photo-style painted illustration (airbrushed toys, not vector cubes), and 1980s toy-packaging type (a chunky italic display with an outline stroke). Lose the soft drop shadows and rounded web cards.
2. Spelling. Big is American: change "Chilli" to "chili", "water biscuits" to "crackers", and "Tick each one off" to "Check each one off". The caviar line "Napkins close by" plus "An acquired taste" is two notes. Cut to one.
3. Dead space. On the front, the left area beside the blocks and the band above "Passed on trays" is empty blue. On the back, the "How to play" band is thin. Fill with box-side copy: a parts list, a safety warning panel, a UPC and a lot number, and a cutout "Party Tray Token" coupon. Small note: the floor piano was in the toy store, not at the party. "For the boss's floor-piano duet" is fine as a wink, but don't imply the party.

## money-train: 7.0, REDO
Object: a kraft paper bakery bag for a Puerto Rican bakery at the subway stairs, 1995. The object is good, and the kraft colour and "Never torched" egg are good. But the train is a flat rounded-square pictogram (a 2010s transit icon), the kraft is a flat tint with no bag construction, and the back ends in empty rules.

Fixes:
1. Replace the train icon with a one-colour rubber-stamp or flexo line drawing of a 1995 New York subway car front (route sign box, storm door, marker lights). Print it in one ink with flexo squash and uneven coverage. Show bag construction: gusset fold lines, a glued seam and a serrated top edge on both faces.
2. Period food. A Michelada was rare in a 1995 Nuyorican bakery, so make it a cold beer with lime or a "cerveza fría" (any added drink can change freely). "Gold lustre" on 1995 token cookies is both British spelling and a 2010s technique: use "a dusting of yellow sugar" if the recipe allows, or at least "luster". "Honey roasted nuts" never mentions honey in the description, so put honey in or rename them.
3. Fill the back. The band between Pedidos and the footer is empty, and so is the bottom 10% below the prices line. Add a hand-stamped date, a "Gracias / Thank You" bag slogan, a token price board, or a pastelito tray drawing. Also Yellowtail is used on Inside Man too, so give this a different script.

## inside-man: 7.9, REDO
Object: a pizza box lid with a taped order ticket (Taken by Stevie, To: Bank, front door, Paid). The idea and the eggs are good: "We're always listening", "Italian and Albanian spoken", "Pizza you can trust". The corrugated stripe is visible. But there is no art at all, and a real 2006 box lid is one-colour flexo print with a stock graphic (a steaming pie, a chef, a skyline), not a full dine-in menu set like a web page.

Fixes:
1. Add flexo box art. A one- or two-ink line drawing (a steaming pie or a lower Manhattan skyline with the bank's columns) with ink squash and dot gain on corrugate, plus the box's die-cut flap lines and a "HOT / FRESH" or "Thank You" band. Push the corrugated texture a touch more so the flutes show through the ink.
2. Fix the period. "Espresso fizz" (espresso and tonic) is a 2010s cafe drink, wrong for a 2006 pizzeria. Make the added drink a period one, or describe it simply as an iced espresso soda if the spec requires it. Potato knishes "rolled in puff pastry" is unusual for a pizzeria knish. Keep the food, but say "wrapped in flaky dough".
3. Change the type. Yellowtail plus Oswald is the same kit as Money Train. Give it a pizzeria flexo look: a heavy italic brush script, uneven ink, and a red that bleeds slightly into the corrugate. Make "2 for 3.92" explain itself or cut it.

## adjustment-bureau: 7.8, REDO
Object: a Norris for Senate election night reception card with drink tickets, Nov. 7, 2006. The eggs are good: Charlie Traynor, the private wedding, "No photographs at the bar", "Mind your shirt". A digital-print look suits 2006 campaign print. But the photo is a smooth duotone and the layout is a stock 2020s template (flat red block, white box, Barlow).

Fixes:
1. Make it a 2006 campaign-printer job. Print the still as a coarse one-colour red halftone, add a real perforation line with tear tooth on the drink tickets, a union print bug-style mark (generic, not a real union logo) next to "Paid for by", and a heavier card with a slight red registration offset.
2. Spelling and food. "centre" should be "center", and "goat's cheese" should be "goat cheese". The Honey-roasted nuts description has no honey (butter, brown sugar, cinnamon), so fix the description or the name. "Cash bar" plus "on us" plus "with our thanks" muddles the tickets. Say the tickets are good for one drink at the bar.
3. Dead space and hierarchy. On the front, the area under the thank-you text to the right of the photo is light. The back is fine but plain. Add a delegate/district results tally grid or a "Call the numbers in to Charlie" box, so the card works on the night.

## 16-blocks: 8.6, REDO
Object: a faxed two-page coffee shop specials and order sheet, March 2006. It's the closest to a real object after Three Men. The fax header, stippled thermal art, the fax-back grid (Nugent, Mosley "no Irish", Bunker holding), birthday cakes and the 16-block map are all well judged.

Fixes:
1. Make it look faxed. A fax prints in black only, so the red handwriting ("Night tour, orders in by 7:30! Sarge", the red grid entries, the red route line) and the yellow highlighter must be black, or clearly added in pen on the received copy. Push the 1-bit look harder: threshold the greys, add a slight skew, scanline streaks and a toner-starved edge.
2. Period and chef notes. "Chopped cheese hero" was a Harlem bodega item that most of Manhattan hadn't heard of until much later. Keep the food but call it a "chopped beef and cheese hero". The Chocolate birthday cake at 2.75 needs the word "slice". "folded round sweet farmer's curd" should be "around".
3. Redraw the map. The 16 blocks are identical rectangles. Give it street names, an avenue label, a varied building row (a church, a bus stop, the bus) and the courthouse steps drawn properly. Avoid a fake real address.

## premium-rush: 8.4, REDO
Object: a messenger company delivery ticket with rates and conditions on the back, 2012. The form is convincing (copy colours, Form DT-3, barcode, typed entries, the totals add up to 41.50). The eggs are good: no brakes, bike cops, Manny owes, "unless dispatch says otherwise".

Fixes:
1. Redraw the zone map. Manhattan is drawn as a sock or cone shape, and the park is a hatched strip on the west edge. Use a correct one-ink line outline of the island with the Hudson and East River shorelines and Central Park in the middle, at the same 59th to 110th band.
2. Chef fixes. "Yellow turmeric underneath" leaves out the rice; it should say "turmeric rice underneath". The Banana berry smoothie description has no banana. Add the carbonless-copy look: a faint second-sheet impression of the handwriting and slightly blurred typewritten fill.
3. Density and finish. Add a pin-feed or tear-off stub with the ticket number on the front, and a rider's copy grease thumbprint or crease. On the back, the three blank dispatch lines and the bottom band are thin. Fill with a phone list or a "Missed signature" box.

## after-hours: 7.9, REDO
Object: a 1985 SoHo bar card with a painted neon sign. The eggs are excellent: the fare went up at midnight, hold on to your twenty, paperweight bagels, plaster of Paris, the ice cream truck, burglaries, Julie and Tom. But a 1985 printed bar card would not carry a photoreal glowing neon render. The front is a screen graphic with a big empty band under the sign, and the back is a modern cream web menu with a fake chalkboard box and a taped note printed in.

Fixes:
1. Print the front the way 1985 would have. Use a one- or two-colour silkscreen or offset of the sign (flat red and cream ink on black stock, maybe a fluorescent spot ink), not a soft glow render. Fill the empty band between the tagline and the info row: an address line, hours or a small line drawing of the block.
2. Make the back one coherent printed sheet. Drop the printed "chalkboard" and "taped notice" effects (they belong on a wall, not a press sheet). Use a typeset 1985 bar menu in condensed gothic and typewriter with the specials as a printed box. Barlow Condensed is a 2017 face, so pick one with an 80s feel.
3. Chef notes. "Paperweight bagels" spells out smoked salmon, capers and the everything seeds, so it reads as a whole bagel spread. Check it matches the recipe and tighten it. Chuck appears in both the burger and the chili. Fine, but vary it to "ground beef" in one. "Vanilla piped high" needs "soft serve" to make sense.
