# STYLE LOCK: the house standard for every film menu (owner approved, Oct 25 2026)

Read this before anything else. It is fixed. Do not invent a new design approach; apply this system to your film. Reference: jurassic/outside.png and jurassic/inside.png. That is what "done" looks like.

## What it is
A real menu, printed by a food venue in the film's world, in that year and place. Beautiful, clean and easy to read. Strong film theming and Easter eggs. Small prop accents are welcome.

## The Jurassic system (copy these exactly, change only the film variables)
1. Paper: one warm paper stock with a soft aged vignette toward the edges, faint fine speckle (shared paper-grain.png), and at most a soft fold line. Nothing over the text. No grime, toner, stains, tape, ditto, skew.
2. Colour: 2 to 3 colours taken from the film's own world (logo, signage, set). One dark, one accent, one paper. Used as flat confident blocks and rules.
3. Type: a bold, clean sans or serif for display (heavy, tight, all caps headlines); a clean readable text face for body. Small spaced-caps kickers in the accent colour above each headline ("WHERE TO EAT AND DRINK"). Dark label bars with paper-coloured text for section titles ("THE MENU"). Nothing else: no typewriter, mono or handwriting faces except one small signature or note.
4. Sizes at 96 dpi: headline 56 to 72px, section title 40 to 48px, dish name 26 to 30px bold, price 26 to 30px bold right-aligned on the same line, description 20 to 22px on one or two lines, kicker 18 to 20px spaced caps, fine print 16px. Nothing under 14px.
5. Menu list: dish name and price on one line, a short plain description under it, a hairline rule between venue groups. Group by the venue's own sections. 8 to 14 items total. Breathing room between items.
6. Hero: the film's own logo (TMDB logo or the venue's in-world sign) plus ONE strong picture: a clean, well-cropped TMDB still in a thin accent frame with a short caption, OR one professionally painted signature illustration (like the island map) that is specific to the film. Never crude shapes, stick figures or flat clip art.
7. Character voice: one short note from a character in the film, with a script signature (Hammond's welcome). One per menu.
8. Fine print footer: "Prices subject to change", a form number or address that hides a film number, the year. Easter egg prices (19.93).
9. Prop accent: at most one or two small, clean props (order ticket, stamp, coupon, matchbook, ticket stub, business card), never over dish text.
10. Format: the real format of that venue's menu. Single card, two-sided card, or a fold-out only if the venue would fold it. US Letter or a real smaller menu size.

## Distinctness (added after the pilot, owner approved)
11. Every film gets its OWN paper colour and palette, taken from the film. Not every menu is cream with black label bars. Change the paper (white, kraft, green, pink, black, coloured stock), the bar shape (black bar, coloured band, outline box, ribbon, ornament) and the display face so no two films look like the same template.
12. Signature art on BOTH faces, at Jurassic level: the front carries the hero (logo or sign plus one strong still or painted illustration); the back carries a second piece (a painted spot illustration, a small map, a framed still, or a bold decorative panel). No face is type only.
13. Art quality bar: if it could pass as clip art, redo it once as a cleaner engraving, painted scene or a TMDB still instead.

## Owner tweaks after pilot round 2 (locked)
14. Bigger, better art: the hero picture fills about a third of the front, with real detail (Jurassic's gate photo and island map are the bar). Small spot art is not enough on its own.
15. Film stills: use at least one real TMDB still on every menu, cleanly cropped in a thin accent frame with a short in-world caption (a venue can show a photo of its street, sign, room or a famous moment that happened there). Bright, well exposed, no muddy tints.
16. Props and Easter eggs: 2 to 3 small props per menu (guest check, stamp, coupon, business card, matchbook, ticket, note) and 6 or more film winks across copy, prices, names and fine print. Props never cover dish text.
17. Every film uses its own display face. Check the other film.json files in wip/print and do not reuse a display face already taken.

## Variety rules (owner approved, Oct 25 evening; overrides rule 10 and the two-face default)
18. FORMAT FOLLOWS THE VENUE. Use the format assigned in your prompt. Formats: landscape placemat (one face, 1344x960), takeaway trifold (two landscape faces, 1344x816, three panels each, fold lines), folded card (two landscape faces 1123x794 outside/inside spread), tall room service folder (two faces 672x1344), single heavy card (one or two faces, 816x1056), slim table card (two faces 408x1056), window or wall card (one face 1056x1344), scorecard fold (two landscape faces 1344x960). Lay out for that format; do not shrink the old two-column letter layout into it.
19. ONE SIGNATURE PIECE per film, like Jurassic's island map: a large, specific, skilled piece only this film could have (a painted map, plan, scene, chart or object). It is assigned in your prompt.
20. PROP QUOTA: guest checks and PAID stamps are now used up. Do not use either unless your prompt allows it. Use the film-specific props in your prompt.
21. LAYOUT: no shared skeleton. Do not default to header, framed still, label bars, two columns. Let the format and signature piece set the layout.

## Real images first (owner, Oct 25 night: "use real images where possible, don't settle")
22. Prefer real photographs and period images over painted ones. Sources, in order: TMDB stills of the film; public domain or free-licence photos from the Library of Congress (loc.gov, tile.loc.gov), NYPL Digital Collections public domain items (images.nypl.org) and Wikimedia Commons (upload.wikimedia.org, public domain, CC0 or CC BY only; never CC BY-SA, NC or ND). Search for real period photos of the venue's street, building, vehicles and food from the film's year and city. Use only images whose page states public domain or a free licence; record the source URL and licence in your folder's CREDITS.md. No visible real brand names or logos in any photo. Paint art only where no usable real image exists, and then at the skill level of the Jurassic map.

## Movie pull (owner, Oct 26, replaces the earlier "movie fun" rule)
23. Every menu must draw you into its film: a strong tie to the film's world and the RIGHT EMOTION for that film. It does not always have to be funny.
   - Match the film's feeling: comedies are funny, crime dramas carry weight and menace, romances are warm, thrillers are tense, family films are playful, epics feel grand, sad films can be tender.
   - 2 or 3 big, unmistakable film moments or motifs a fan recognises in seconds (an object, a place, a scene, a visual motif), on top of the small winks.
   - Copy written in the film's own tone and voice (short paraphrased winks, never song lyrics, never long quotes).
   - Props and pictures chosen to pull you into the world, not just paperwork.
   It must never feel like a polite generic restaurant with the film's name on it.

## Design-led (owner, Oct 26: Beetlejuice graded 4.5, batch 6 "ok-ish")
24. Use the film's OWN visual language as the design system: its palette, cinematography, production design, title design and iconic motifs (Burton stripes and crooked type, Willis shadows and amber, Vegas neon, Leone sepia). Do not settle for a neat generic layout with film details pasted on. The front should feel like a frame from the film. Signature art large and skilled.

## Film variables (the only things that change per film)
Venue and its name, palette, display face, logo or sign, hero picture, section names, dishes and wording, character note, Easter eggs, prop accent, format.

## Process limits (to protect the owner's budget)
- One build pass, then one self-check and fix. Do not iterate beyond that.
- Do not research more than 2 quick searches. Do not redo art more than once.
- Keep build.py simple: HTML faces plus at most one painted art PNG.
- Report in under 200 words.
