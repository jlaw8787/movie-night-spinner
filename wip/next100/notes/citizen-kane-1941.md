# Citizen Kane (1941)

Layout: newspaper (kept). Venue: Inquirer Welcome Dinner, the New York Inquirer (the plan's lead, narrowed to the scene).

## Venue
The plan's lead, the Inquirer, holds up. Kane buys the New York Inquirer (Wikipedia) and throws a welcome for the newspapermen he has hired away from the Chronicle: "Welcome, gentlemen, to the Inquirer! ... our circulation this morning was the greatest in New York, 684,000." Bernstein: "Six hundred and eighty-four thousand one hundred and thirty-two!" (Wikiquote). The sign uses that number. The name is descriptive, not a name from the film.

## Facts and quotes used
Wikipedia (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizen_Kane):
- Last word "Rosebud"; the sled in the Colorado snow; Rosebud is the sled's trade name (pavlova line, wink, weather ear "Snow in Colorado. Bring the sled.").
- Kane finishes and prints Leland's bad review of Susan's opera debut and fires him (wink, badge "Drama critic: J. Leland / Fired by Kane").
- Susan is now a nightclub owner and refuses to talk to Thompson (drink line).
- Music section: the Everglades picnic, framing the fight in the tent between Susan and Kane (fried chicken line).
Wikiquote (https://en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Citizen_Kane):
- Note, Kane: "If the headline is big enough, it makes the news big enough." (verbatim).
- Tagline "The classic story of power and the press." (T).
- Leland: "You still eating?" Kane: "I'm still hungry." (lobster name and line, cue).
- Leland on the Declaration of Principles (oysters).
- Welcome speech and the men who were with the Chronicle (sirloin line, house champagne note).
- Kane: "You can't buy a bag of peanuts in this town without someone writing a song about you." (house peanuts).
- Leland: "Candidate Kane found in love nest with quote, singer, unquote." (wink).
- Susan: "Forty-nine thousand acres of nothing but scenery and statues." (wink).
- Reporters: "Another Venus." (wink); Susan's "a statue you're gonna keep crated up" (wink wording "still in its crate").

## Unsure
- "Hires the Chronicle's best men": Wikiquote shows Bernstein calling them "the best men in the business" who were with the Chronicle; Kane's line is about the picture of them six years earlier.
- The welcome scene is a celebration at the Inquirer; whether it is a dinner (food on tables) I did not confirm. The venue name is descriptive.
- Lobster, oysters and sirloin are period dishes, not food seen on screen; their lines claim the scene only.
