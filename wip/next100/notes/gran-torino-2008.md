# Gran Torino (2008)

Layout: home (kept). Venue: The Vang Lor Family Barbecue, next door to Walt's in Metro Detroit (the plan's lead, "Thao and Sue's family barbecue", named for the family as Wikipedia does). Hmong-style dishes from the family, plus Walt's own kielbasa and his Blue Ribbon on the porch.

## Venue
- Wikipedia: Walt's next-door neighbours are "the Vang Lor family"; his neighbourhood is in Metro Detroit. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gran_Torino
- GradeSaver summary: "Sue invites Walt to a barbecue next door; Walt joins because he is out of beer." https://www.gradesaver.com/gran-torino/study-guide/summary
- Plugged In: "Sue invites him over for a barbecue." https://www.pluggedin.com/?p=16249

## Facts and quotes used
Wikiquote (https://en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Gran_Torino):
- Note, Walt: "Get off my lawn!"
GradeSaver (https://www.gradesaver.com/gran-torino/study-guide/summary):
- Walt goes to the barbecue because he is out of beer (sausage line, cue).
- At the house he learns Hmong people don't touch others on the head (papaya salad line).
- "Members of the Hmong community drop off gifts and food on Walt's porch in the following days." (skewers line).
Plugged In (https://www.pluggedin.com/?p=16249):
- Walt smokes and drinks "Blue Ribbon beer", with "several empty cans" stacked beside him (drink name and line).
Wikipedia (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gran_Torino, plot read raw):
- Walt is a Polish American, retired Ford factory worker (kielbasa name and line).
- His 1972 Ford Gran Torino; he leaves it to Thao on condition Thao does not modify it (dessert line, prop, number 1972).
- Daisy the Labrador, Thao's construction job, the Silver Star, Father Janovich (winks).

## Unsure
- No source names the food at the barbecue; the Hmong-style dishes (sausage, papaya salad, lemongrass skewers, sticky rice, tapioca) are menu dishes and the lines claim only the scenes.
- Plugged In says "Blue Ribbon beer"; I wrote Pabst Blue Ribbon in full.
- I left Wikiquote's slur-heavy lines off the menu on purpose.
- House lines (prawn crackers, jasmine tea) are plain menu jokes.
