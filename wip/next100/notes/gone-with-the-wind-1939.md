# Gone with the Wind (1939)

Layout: banquet (kept). Venue: The Twelve Oaks Barbecue, the Wilkes plantation, Georgia, 1861 (the plan's lead).

## Venue
Wikipedia: the engagement party "the next day at Ashley's home, Twelve Oaks", disrupted by news of Fort Sumter. Filmsite calls it "the neighboring Twelve Oaks plantation's barbecue" and describes carriages drawing up "for the lavish Wilkes barbecue".
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gone_with_the_Wind_(film) (plot, read raw)

## Facts and quotes used
Wikipedia (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gone_with_the_Wind_(film)):
- 1861; Ashley to marry Melanie, the party at Twelve Oaks (wink "Ashley and Melanie, just engaged").
- Rhett walks away into the early morning fog (julep line).
Wikiquote (https://en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Gone_with_the_Wind_(film)):
- Note, Scarlett: "After all... tomorrow... is another day!" printed as "After all, tomorrow is another day!" (punctuation smoothed); house muffins note.
- "As God is my witness, I'll never be hungry again!" (radish name and line).
- Scarlett: "Fiddle-dee-dee. War, war, war; this war talk's spoiling all the fun at every party this spring." (cobbler line).
- "Frankly, my dear, I don't give a damn." (julep name, wink).
- Tagline "The greatest romance of all time!" (T).
Filmsite (https://www.filmsite.org/gone.html, gone2.html, gone3.html):
- Mammy laces Scarlett up before the Twelve Oaks barbecue and makes her eat first (wink, still caption).
- At the Monster Bazaar in Atlanta Rhett bids "$150 'in gold'" for the opening dance with Scarlett in mourning (devilled eggs line, place card).
- Scarlett digs up a radish at dawn at Tara, then makes the vow (radish line).
- Mammy sews a dress from Tara's green velvet drapes (lamb name and line, wink).
- Carriages draw up at Twelve Oaks for the lavish Wilkes barbecue (cue).

## Unsure
- None of the food is shown at the barbecue on screen as far as the sources say; all dishes are themed and their lines claim the scene only.
- The quote's punctuation is smoothed from Wikiquote's "After all...tomorrow...is another day!".
- The film's treatment of slavery is left off the menu on purpose; nothing on the card jokes about it.
