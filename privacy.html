Privacy policy

Last updated 16 November 2026

The short version. You can use Reel Dinner without an account, on a guest pass. Your picks stay on your device unless you choose to sign in for a member card. There are no ads and no tracking cookies. The website counts visits with GoatCounter, which keeps no personal data, and nothing is sold or shared for marketing.

What stays on your device

Reel Dinner saves these in your browser or app storage, on your device only:

  • Films you have marked as watched, your favourites and your star ratings
  • Your spin history, weekly plan, shopping ticks and pantry items
  • Your settings, such as diet, guests, sound and kids mode
  • Your member card number and the name you type on it, if you add one

You can clear all of it at any time from Settings, or by clearing the site data in your browser.

Member accounts (optional)

If you tap "Get your member card" and sign in, we keep your card in your account so it is the same on every device you sign in on. You can sign in with an emailed code or with Google. There is no password.

Your account holds your email address (and, if you use Google, the basic profile Google shares: your name and email), your member number, the name on your card if you added one, and your watched list, favourites, ratings, history, weekly plan, your rentals (how many, which aisles, and the tapes you have out), the treats you ticked at the counter, your Match night "Just me" shortlist and the films you have already swiped, and your kids mode and skip watched films settings.

Signing up asks you to confirm you are 18 or over, because the menus include alcohol.

It is stored with our database provider, Supabase, on servers in Sydney. Emails are sent for us by Resend. We use your email to sign you in and, if you leave them switched on, to send the friend alerts below. We do not send marketing, and we do not sell or share it.

Friends and Dinner Duel

Members can add friends with an invite link or a member number, and send each other Dinner Duels. Friends see the name on your member card (or your member number if you leave it blank). They never see your email address.

For this we keep your friends list, friend requests, anyone you block, the duels you play (the films in each deck, what each of you kept or skipped, and the match), invite links you make (they stop working after two weeks), your inbox of turns, matches and requests, and your alert settings. The person you duel only sees what you kept once neither of you has matched and it's time for the tie-breaker.

Emails. When a friend sends you a duel, you match, someone asks to be friends, or a duel sits for two days (the joke late fee), we may email you. One email a day at most, never between 9pm and 8am Brisbane time, plus a Monday catch-up only if a friend is waiting on you. Switch each off in My account, Settings, or use the link at the bottom of any email.

Blocking someone removes them, hides what they sent you and stops their links, requests and duels reaching you. Closed duels are deleted after 60 days, and inbox lines you have read after 90 days.

Signing out. Signing out puts the device back on a guest pass and clears your picks from that device. Your card stays in your account for next time.

Deleting it. Open My account (the card button at the top) and go to Settings, or open your member card in Galaxy Video, and choose "Delete my account". Your account and everything in it, including your friends, duels and inbox, is removed straight away, on every device.

Older cards. Before accounts, you could put a card online with a member number and a 4-digit PIN. Those cards keep working, with a scrambled copy of the PIN (we cannot read it). When you sign in on a device with one of those cards, it moves into your account and the PIN is removed. "Delete my online card" still removes an older card.

Your choices

You can ask us what we hold about your account or online member card, ask us to correct it, or ask us to delete it. Email us from the address you sign in with, or with your member number, and we will sort it out. If you are in Australia and are not happy with our answer, you can contact the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner.

If this policy changes, we will update this page and the date at the top.
