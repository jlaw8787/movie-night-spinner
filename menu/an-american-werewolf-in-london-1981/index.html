Snacks

Slaughtered Lamb pork crackling

Dry-brined pork rind roasted until blistered and crisp, with flaky salt

Prep 15 min, cook 750 min · Make ahead: Dry the rind overnight. Best eaten the same day
Recipe for 4
  • 600g pork rind
  • 2 tbsp sea salt flakes
  1. Score the rind and rub with flaky salt.
  2. Dry uncovered in the fridge overnight.
  3. Roast at 240C until blistered and crisp all over.
  4. Cool, then break into pieces. Stay on the road.

Slaughtered Lamb Scotch eggs

Soft boiled eggs wrapped in sausage, crumbed and fried, with English mustard

Prep 30 min, cook 12 min · Make ahead: Up to 1 day chilled
Recipe for 4
  • 5 eggs
  • 400g pork sausage mince
  • 1/2 cup plain flour
  • 100g panko breadcrumbs
  • 2 tbsp English mustard
  • 1l vegetable oil
  1. Boil four eggs, cool and peel.
  2. Wrap each in seasoned sausage mince.
  3. Roll in flour, the beaten egg and panko.
  4. Deep fry in the vegetable oil at 170C until deep golden.
  5. Halve and serve with English mustard.

Mains

Moors shepherd's pie

Lamb mince in tomato and Worcestershire gravy under baked mash. Not a meatloaf, Jack.

Prep 25 min, cook 85 min · Make ahead: The lamb filling can be made 2 days ahead; top with mash and bake on the night
Recipe for 4
  • 750g lamb mince
  • 1 brown onion, diced
  • 2 carrots, diced
  • 2 tbsp tomato paste
  • 2 tbsp Worcestershire sauce
  • 250ml beef stock
  • 1kg potatoes
  • 60g butter
  1. Brown the lamb.
  2. Add the onion, carrots, tomato paste, Worcestershire and stock and simmer.
  3. Boil the potatoes and mash with butter.
  4. Top the lamb with mash and bake at 200C until golden.

East Proctor beer-battered haddock

Haddock in crisp ale batter with thick-cut chips, minty mushy peas and malt vinegar

Prep 25 min, cook 35 min · Make ahead: Par-cook the chips up to 4 hours ahead and crush the peas up to 1 day ahead; batter and fry the fish just before serving
Recipe for 4
  • 4 skinless haddock or flathead fillets
  • 200g self-raising flour
  • 300ml cold pale ale
  • 1kg floury potatoes
  • 1 1/2l vegetable oil, for frying
  • 500g frozen peas
  • 30g butter
  • 1 bunch mint
  • 1 lemon
  • malt vinegar
  1. Cut the potatoes into thick chips, simmer 5 minutes, drain and dry well.
  2. Fry the chips at 160°C for 6 minutes, lift out and rest.
  3. Whisk the flour with the cold ale and a pinch of salt into a thick batter.
  4. Dust the fish in a little extra flour, dip in the batter and fry at 180°C for 5 to 6 minutes until golden. Keep warm.
  5. Fry the chips again at 190°C until crisp.
  6. Simmer the peas 5 minutes, drain and crush with the butter, chopped mint and salt. Serve with lemon and malt vinegar.

Sweets

Piccadilly jam roly-polyVegetarian

Baked jam roll pudding served with hot custard

Prep 25 min, cook 55 min · Make ahead: Bake up to 1 day ahead and reheat wrapped in foil at 160C for 15 minutes; heat the custard on the night
Recipe for 4
  • 250g self-raising flour
  • 125g cold butter, grated
  • 30g caster sugar
  • 150ml water
  • 200g raspberry jam
  • 500ml vanilla custard
  1. Mix the flour, butter, sugar and water into a dough.
  2. Roll to a rectangle and spread with jam.
  3. Roll up.
  4. Bake at 180C.
  5. Heat the custard and serve alongside.

Drinks

Slaughtered Lamb mulled aleVegetarian

English ale warmed with spices and orange, with brandy stirred in

Prep 5 min, cook 15 min · Make ahead: Best heated just before serving; measure the sugar and spices into the saucepan up to 1 day ahead
Recipe for 4
  • 2l English ale (4 bottles of 500 ml)
  • 3 tbsp brown sugar
  • 1 cinnamon stick
  • 4 cloves
  • 2 strips orange peel
  • 1/4 tsp nutmeg, freshly grated
  • 80ml brandy
  1. Pour the ale into a saucepan with the sugar, cinnamon, cloves, orange peel and nutmeg.
  2. Heat gently, keeping it well under a simmer so the alcohol stays.
  3. Stir in the brandy.
  4. Strain into heatproof mugs and serve steaming.
