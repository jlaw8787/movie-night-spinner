# Third independent review

**Overall: 7.5 / 10**

A genuinely charming, well made site with a strong cinema identity and some printed menus that are better than most real restaurant menus. What holds it back is a handful of visible seams: the prep sheet quietly drops dishes that the printed menu advertises, the Galaxy Video aisle signs miss their sign boards, the candy bar leaks Galaxy Video into the cinema, and desktop wastes half the screen once a film is open.

Tested 3 November 2026 on a local copy. Real screenshots at 390x844 (phone, touch, 2x) and 1440x900 (desktop). Films checked closely: Wall Street, The Lost City, Two Hands, Indiana Jones and the Last Crusade, Harry Potter and the Order of the Phoenix, Ghost Dog, You've Got Mail, Rocky Balboa, Solo, The Good the Bad and the Ugly, Boomerang, Rogue One, plus spin results Peter Rabbit 2 and Mad Max Beyond Thunderdome. That covers bistro, invite, bar tab, tavern, home recipe box, newspaper, galley, saloon, hotel, canteen, market, chalkboard and two hand-built menus.

## 1. First impression and visual identity (8)

The marquee photo with the film title set in perspective on the letter board is the best thing on the site. During a spin it ticks through titles live, then lands on the winner. The gold bulb wheel, red curtain and deco pattern all sit in the same world. The welcome pop-up is short, plain and useful.

What costs points:
- Desktop 1440x900: the marquee takes the top 390px, so the Spin button sits at about y 800 and the bottom of the wheel is cut off. The first thing you're meant to press is barely on screen.
- Phone: marquee plus nav is about 520 of 844px, Spin button half visible. Acceptable, but tight.
- The red curtain placeholder ("Spin the wheel to raise the curtain") is a 640px tall block on phone and a large one on desktop that does nothing until you spin.

## 2. Brand consistency (7)

Mostly well kept. Switching to Galaxy Video recolours the shared nav purple and gold, and the store has its own type, colours and voice. The cinema never uses the purple palette on its own pages.

Where it leaks:
- **Candy bar, "Spin a combo"**: opens a full Galaxy Video hire case (purple header, teal and pink stripe, marker-font "Staff tip", **Rent it** button) on top of the red and gold candy bar. That is the store inside the cinema.
- Cinema result page copy: "Film 2 of the 2 Peter Rabbit films in the store" and "Film 3 of the 5 Mad Max films in the store". "Store" is Galaxy Video language.
- Footer on Galaxy Video credits the cinema photo first. Minor, but it's the one shared surface.

## 3. Printed menus (8)

The hand-built ones are excellent and should not be touched (see below). The shared layouts are strong as a system: each one reads as a believable object from the film's world, uses real stills, and the prices, form numbers and in-jokes are consistent within a menu. The Two Hands bar tab carries 57.75 over from page one and the running tab adds up to 75.75 correctly. That kind of care is rare.

Recurring seams in the shared layouts:
- **Duplicate logo**: poster already carries the title, then a second logo block sits beside or below it (Two Hands, Harry Potter, Rocky Balboa, Good the Bad and the Ugly, Boomerang, Solo, Indiana Jones, Rogue One).
- **Dead space where content runs short**: Wall Street page one has a big empty field above and below the title block; The Lost City has "YOU'RE INVITED! NOW SHOWING" stranded in a tall empty column; Solo has one main under "Ration 2" and a bare half page; Rocky page two has an empty band at the bottom.
- **Uneven type sizes**: items without descriptions print small and items with descriptions print large on the same page (Solo, Good the Bad and the Ugly, Boomerang right page at about 9px equivalent while the top third is near empty).
- **Filler or flavour-only descriptions**: Rogue One "Jedha spiced lamb pilaf: Lamb." and "Za'atar flatbreads with hummus: Grilled za'atar flatbreads with hummus."; Peter Rabbit "Creamy chicken.", "Strawberry, rockmelon."; Wall Street shows "Bud's flashy new apartment" under Sushi platter while the real description ("salmon and tuna nigiri, cucumber maki") sits unused in the data.
- **Repeated or clumsy footer lines**: Good the Bad and the Ugly prints "Talk of the town: Sad Hill Cemetery. Talk of the town: Blondie's poncho."; Wall Street prints "Gekko and Co. Dining Room" then "Gekko and Co. Power Lunch" straight under it.
- **Odd venue choices**: Boomerang's in-room dining card is branded "Lady Eloise Cosmetics", a cosmetics company, not a place that serves food. Rogue One's canteen header uses character names as timetable slots ("9:00 Jyn Erso and the Death Star").
- **Spelling mix for an Australian site**: "sweet chili" and "chilli" on the same Boomerang menu, "coconut prawns" described as "shrimp" (Lost City, Rogue One), "soft center" on Two Hands, a Sydney film. In the data, "shrimp" appears in 47 film files and "center" in 14. In-world American venues can keep American words, but the Aussie films and the descriptions shouldn't.
- Tiny print that can't be read at any size: "NOW SHOWING 1966" on Good the Bad and the Ugly.

## 4. Phone experience (7)

Good:
- Opening a film scrolls straight to the printed menu. Page one shows near full width, with dots and "Swipe to turn, tap to zoom".
- The full size viewer pages cleanly with a swipe, has Page 1 of 2 controls, and Escape closes it.
- The text menu under the printed one takes the film's own fonts and colours, with Starters, Mains, Sweets, Drinks tabs and an expandable numbered method per dish. It reads well.

Not good:
- **Landscape layouts are unreadable on phone.** Rogue One (canteen) fills a 390px wide strip with menu text around 6px, half the viewer is empty, and the only help is "Turn your phone sideways". Good the Bad and the Ugly items are about 7px. The text menu saves you, but the printed menu is the product.
- The viewer adds little over the inline view on phone: same fit-width scale, with roughly 110px of empty black above and below.
- To get from the printed menu to the Prep tab you scroll back up past programme controls, four buttons and the Menu of the week card. The Menu of the week card stays between the controls and the open film, which is clutter once a film is chosen.
- Galaxy Video on phone is about 15,000px tall and the member card, a headline feature, sits at the very bottom above the footer.
- Candy bar on phone shows an empty beige strip right under the photo (see fix 3).
- The page is 391px wide at a 390px viewport, so there's a 1px sideways wobble.

## 5. Core flow (7)

Spin is satisfying: about 6.7 seconds, ticks, the pointer kicks, the marquee follows. Search is quick with clear empty results ("No film by that name. Try fewer letters."). Methods are properly quantified with per-step timers, make-ahead notes and sensible scaling: 10 guests gives 3 egg yolks, 3 oranges and 60 oysters, not 2.5 and 7.5.

Problems:
- **The prep sheet drops dishes the menu advertises, and says it hasn't.** Wall Street's printed menu lists 9 dishes; the prep sheet and Choose dishes list only 5 and read "5 of 5 dishes picked". Sushi platter, Parker House rolls, Carl's cheeseburger and Bluestar espresso tonic can't be added, even though the phone text menu has a method for each. Two Hands: 9 printed, 5 in prep. Peter Rabbit 2: 7 printed, 5 in prep. Someone who wants the cheeseburger has no way to get it on the shopping list.
- **Spin button ignores presses for about 2 seconds after landing.** At 7 seconds after a spin a second click does nothing; at 9 seconds it works. The button shows as enabled the whole time. It happened with Enter, Space and mouse.
- **Desktop doesn't bring the result into view.** After a spin the page stays at the top; the menu starts at y 650, so you see a sliver of poster and have to scroll to find out what you got.
- **Desktop prep sheet is squeezed into the right column** at about 580px wide in small Cormorant on near black, while the left column sits empty for roughly 3,000px under the wheel.
- Shopping list wording: "4 ribs of eye steaks" (should be "4 rib eye steaks"); fractions print as "2 1/2 tbsp".
- "Every recipe serves four" on the text menu sits awkwardly next to the welcome pop-up's "scales from 1 to 60 guests". Say "Serves 4, change it on the Prep tab".

## 6. Galaxy Video store (7)

The concept and voice are terrific: face-out tapes with rating badges, "All copies out. Lucky you, one just came back", $2 Tuesday actually running on a Tuesday with the $7 overnight price struck through, a Melbourne Cup aisle on Cup day with Phar Lap and The Man from Snowy River, "a chook with a rider" on the Minecraft case. The hire case reads like the back of a box, and See the menu, Rent it, Seen it are clear.

Problems:
- **Aisle signs miss the sign boards on the desktop interior photo.** Only "New releases" lands on its hanging board. "Comedy" floats below an empty board; "Family", "Horror", "Sci-fi" and "Drama" are small purple words floating over the shelves with no sign behind them, while four real blank boards hang empty. It looks broken.
- The walk-in shows the shopfront photo, then you walk in and the same shopfront photo is the first thing in the store.
- Member card: "TAP TO ADD YOUR NAME" sits on top of the fine print "Card remains the property of Galaxy Video", so the two overlap. "Put my card online" and "Use my card from another device" don't tell you what either does.
- The price board says "Friday night: one for the kids, one for later" under "On tonight" on a Tuesday.
- Desktop: the price board floats with an empty area above it beside the member card.
- Phone hire case: the cover sits below the fold under the text. On a video box the cover is the hook; lead with it, or at least a thumbnail.

## 7. Polish and craft (7)

The art direction is consistent and the small Aussie touches land (Cheerios with tomato sauce, musk sticks, choc tops, chicken salt). Copy is mostly plain and warm.

Polish misses beyond those above:
- Desktop copy still says "Tap" in places ("Tap a treat", "Tap something on the board", "Tap to add your name").
- Candy bar board on desktop: five columns don't fill the width and the popcorn graphic overlaps the last column.
- Candy bar counts look inflated: Seafood 298 films, a third of the catalogue.
- Desktop Menu of the week card changes width and alignment after a spin (full width before, a narrower centred pill after, not aligned with the column below).
- Guest stepper uses a hyphen "-" for minus on the Prep tab (Settings uses a proper minus).
- First load moves about 2.3MB on phone (index.html 269KB, providers.js 360KB, app.js 337KB). Fine on wifi, worth trimming for mobile data.

## 8. Accessibility basics (8)

Better than most hobby sites by a distance:
- Skip link works and is first in the tab order.
- Focus rings are visible on every control, in gold, with good contrast.
- Tab order through the box office is logical: nav, mute, Spin, programme, search, the four buttons, Menu of the week, checklist, footer.
- Enter spins, and focus moves to the result heading afterwards.
- Settings traps focus, Escape closes and returns focus to the Settings button.
- The split-letter "MOVIE NIGHT" h1 has an aria-label; the wheel canvas has role img and a label; all images have alt attributes; sampled text contrast is 7:1 or better.

Gaps:
- The silent post-spin dead zone (fix 6) also hits keyboard and screen reader users: nothing announces that the press was ignored.
- Footer links are about 19px tall, below a comfortable touch target.
- Prep sheet body text is light Cormorant at about 14px on near black on desktop, readable but tiring for a long method.
- The printed menu has no text equivalent on desktop; phone gets the text menu, desktop only gets the image-like card and the viewer.

## Genuinely excellent, leave it alone

- The marquee with the live film title on the letter board, including the ticking during a spin.
- The hand-built menus. Ghost Dog's Raymond's ice cream board (bilingual, rooftop still, Rashomon library card) and You've Got Mail's Fox and Sons cafe menu (FB-152, free hot cocoa clip, staff pick Pride and Prejudice) are as good as anything on the site.
- Wall Street, Two Hands and Rocky Balboa among the shared layouts: strong objects, good stills, and the Two Hands tab maths actually adds up.
- The phone swipe viewer and the themed phone text menu with expandable methods.
- Prep sheet methods: quantified, timed steps, make-ahead notes, and rounding that a cook would do.
- Galaxy Video's live details: $2 Tuesday on a Tuesday with the struck-through price, the Melbourne Cup aisle on Cup day, "All copies out. Lucky you, one just came back".
- The Aussie snack counter content (Cheerios, fairy bread, chocolate crackles, choc tops, chicken salt).
- Keyboard and focus handling, and the Settings sheet. Clear, honest copy ("Saved on this device. Reset clears...").
