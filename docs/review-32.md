# Review 32: Reel Dinner

**Overall: 7.5 out of 10**

Reel Dinner looks and works like a finished product. The cinema front is rich and confident, Galaxy Video is a properly separate shop with its own look, the spin flow lands cleanly, the phone printed menus now open on the food page and most of them can be read, and the Google film pages are clean. The hand-built menus (about 97) are excellent, close to the 9.5 target.

What keeps the score down is the other 900. The shared library layouts carry most of the catalogue, and when you read them in full the content does not hold up to the owner's standards. The printed notes are often trimmed into stubs. A lot of menus have no currency, or the wrong one. Period prices are off by a factor of ten in places. Cheap bites cost more than real dishes. The bar tab arithmetic is wrong on about half the bar tab menus. Template wording bleeds into venues it does not fit. None of these is hard to fix one at a time, but together they turn up in most library menus you open. The fact checking, quotes and venues are mostly good, which shows the content pipeline can get there.

## What is strong

- **The cinema front.** The marquee photo, the gilt wheel with bulbs, the red and gold ticket buttons and the Admit One stub make a strong first impression (review-32-01.png). The wheel spins and lands on the film under the pointer, and the menu drops in beside it (review-32-01b.png).
- **Hand-built menus are a pleasure.** Jurassic Park's dining guide ($19.93 lamb, "Hold on to your butts." on the coffee), Die Hard's Nakatomi party card (correct that 24 December 1988 was a Saturday, the tip from the man in 4B), Back to the Future's Lou's Cafe (Saturday 5 November 1955 is correct), Louis' in the Bronx for The Godfather ("The best veal in the city", Clemenza's line about sugar), Kung Fu Panda priced in coins, Lady and the Tramp at Tony's. These read like real menus from the film's world.
- **Venues and facts are mostly right.** The Crown (Hot Fuzz), The Winchester, L Street Tavern, the Slaughtered Lamb, Caffè Rocca on Piazza della Rotonda (Roman Holiday), Café des 2 Moulins with francs (Amélie), Turkish and Tommy's arcade (Snatch), the Frosty Palace, the 20th Century Limited for The Sting, Changchun wedding banquet (The Farewell), Goode's lunch room with pre-decimal prices (Ladies in Black), won on Train to Busan, lire on Roman Holiday, pence on The Witch and A Christmas Carol.
- **Notes written as notes.** Many library menus now put invented words in a note voice rather than a signed quote: Thomasin in The Witch, Fezziwig in A Christmas Carol (a real Dickens line), Red's rooftop note in Shawshank, Lady Sarah in Australia.
- **Real quotes are mostly real.** "Wouldst thou like the taste of butter?", "Tell me about it, stud.", "Well, I can't make love to a bush!", "Rudeness is merely an expression of fear.", "We all go a little mad sometimes.", "Me fifth element, supreme being. Me protect you.", "But why is the rum gone?", "A true selfless act always sparks another."
- **Phone printed menu.** It opens on the food page in all 15 films I tried (Hot Fuzz, Shaun, Gallipoli, Wall-E, Train to Busan, Paddington 2 and so on). Wide pages split into panels on clear gutters, so no word is cut (Wall-E in three panels, Jurassic Park in six). Jurassic Park and Die Hard are very readable (review-32-02.png, review-32-14.png, review-32-15.png). The big-type text menu underneath is clear, with course tabs and "How to make it" buttons.
- **Prep sheet.** A clear shopping list by aisle, a "bottle shop" section, timed steps and make-ahead notes for each dish. Save PDF gives three sensible choices with print advice (review-32-08.png).
- **No em dashes** in any of the 1,000 rendered menus, the app code I checked, or the 1,000 film pages.
- **Brands kept apart.** Cinema screens are red and gold throughout, including the guest pass (review-32-12.png). Galaxy Video is purple, gold, teal and pink right through to its member card dialog and tape backs (review-32-09.png, review-32-10.png).
- **Quiz night on the phone.** All four answers sat on screen for all ten questions, the dots track right and wrong, and the score card is tidy (review-32-11.png).
- **Film pages.** 1,000 pages, unique titles, canonical links, valid JSON-LD with WebPage, BreadcrumbList and an ItemList of 5,357 Recipe objects. Every recipe has an image, ingredients and steps, and no total time is shorter than prep plus cook.

## What holds it back

1. **Library notes trimmed to stubs on the printed menu.** The phone text menu has the full note, but the printed card cuts it short. Hot Fuzz prints "Beef braised in ale." and "Whole bird roasted golden, potatoes." Wallace & Gromit: Vengeance Most Fowl prints "Wensleydale and cheddar.", "Egg-dipped bread fingers." and "Gromit's beloved vegetable garden." Babe prints "Pecorino, sheep's feta." and "Potato and leek." Strictly Ballroom prints "Slow cooked potato.", "Butter basted chicken." and "Rico shows Scott the real paso doble out." Boy prints "Milk, vanilla ice cream." and "Vanilla ice cream folded through." (folded through what?), and its "Homebrew ginger beer" has no note at all. What We Do in the Shadows prints "Thick double-fried chips." and "Rare sirloin sliced thin." My count found at least 400 printed notes of four words or fewer across about 200 films. That is before counting notes that are only a film reference with no dish, like "Rudi, who proposes to Fay in his new flat." (Ladies in Black), "Farrat takes a feather boa as a bribe." (The Dressmaker), "Mr Ping's Secret Ingredient Soup, no peeking." on a silken tofu dish (Kung Fu Panda 2).
2. **Money.** In the 114 menus I read closely, 51 print bare numbers with no currency (".25", "1.75", "89.50"). Where the money is shown, it is sometimes the wrong money. Young Frankenstein charges dollars in Transylvania. The Great Escape charges cents in Stalag Luft III. Oliver! prints "TWO CENTS" on a London masthead. The Hogwarts welcome feast is "£51.25" per cover instead of Galleons. Snow White's cottage charges "35¢". Shrek, The Lion King and Moana use bare decimals instead of storybook money. The banquet layout's "The whole feast, per cover" shows "1.75" (Gone with the Wind), "89.50" (Dune), "1.70" (Mulan), "3.40" (The Wizard of Oz) with nothing to say what money it is.
3. **Period prices.** The Prestige (1890s London) charges 7/9 for a chicken pie and 2/8 for cucumber sandwiches, about ten times too dear for the period. An American Werewolf in London (1981) charges £3.00 a pint. Mad Max (1979) has a $4.25 meat pie. The Dish (Parkes, 1969) charges $1.00 for a scone. Red Dog prints "EST. 2011" and "Licence 2011" but charges $0.90 for a radler and $1.50 for a sausage roll.
4. **Cheap bites dearer than real dishes.** Crocodile Dundee: pork crackling $5.75 against garlic prawn skewers $3.75 and barramundi $6.25. Lost in Translation: a "Wagyu katsu sando" at ¥410 is the cheapest thing on a Park Hyatt menu. Two Hands: potato scallops $8.25 against fish and chips $6.50. Deadpool: "Pickled eggs from the jar" $9.75 and samosas $18.00 in a dive bar. Mad Max: pub pickled onions $3.00 against chips $1.95. Psycho: pickled onions 35¢ against sandwiches 25¢.
5. **Bar tab sums are wrong.** The bar tab layout carries a total over the fold. In 17 of the 35 bar tab menus it does not add up. Deadpool items add to 106.25 but print "CARRIED OVER 114.50". Star Wars adds to 16.45 but prints 15.95. An American Werewolf in London adds to £26.70 but prints £27.75. Top Gun: Maverick prints 114.10 for items that add to 118.50. Goldfinger's scorecard total prints "£1 11S" (the items add to £1 11s 4d).
6. **Template wording bleeds into venues.** Next Goal Wins puts a village pitch in American Samoa under a stadium map with "CLUB LEVEL" and "SUITES". Boy (Waihau Bay, 1984) is headed "PROM NIGHT, CLASS OF 2010". The Palace Hotel in Broken Hill (Priscilla) gets the diner line "Pie so good it ought to be a crime." Kung Fu Hustle (1940s Pig Sty Alley) offers "Free delivery over $25" and a phone number, when the whole meal for two costs 2.50. Ben's Famous Pizza in Men in Black II is printed as a lab ration sheet with "ACCESS LV 02". The saloon layout prints "REWARD £1942" (Australia) and "REWARD £82" next to a "100 POUND REWARD" card (The Man from Snowy River). Grease says "ASK ABOUT PIE: Frosty Palace burger, 4.50" while the menu prices the burger at 1.50.
7. **Invented lines signed as quotes.** Hot Fuzz prints "Sandford is the safest village in England. Have a cuppa, Nick." signed Danny Butterman in script. Notting Hill prints "Well, you'll have to fight me for it. This is a very good brownie." in quote marks signed William Thacker. Gladiator joins two separate Maximus lines into one quote. These are fewer than in the past, but they break the owner's rule directly.
8. **Modern food in period settings.** Mary Poppins (1910) serves seeded crackers with whipped goat's cheese. Australia (1939) serves lemon myrtle barramundi with mango salsa and coconut rice, and a wattleseed pavlova. Gladiator serves tomato chickpeas in Rome. It Happened One Night (1934) has buttermilk ranch dip and baby carrots. Beneath Hill 60 has macadamia Anzac biscuits. Storm Boy (1976) has lemon myrtle salt. One or two are fine; together they make the period menus feel generic.
9. **Some phone menus are not readable when they open.** Ratatouille shows the whole Gusteau's menu at about 5 to 6 pixel text and says "Tap the menu to read it full size" (review-32-03.png). Gone with the Wind's bill of fare opens at about 7 pixel italic (review-32-04.png). Grease opens on page 3, "The Menu, continued", not the page with the starters and burgers (review-32-05.png). On Hot Fuzz the pub fascia band at the top of the food panel is empty, because "THE CROWN, SANDFORD" falls on the other panel (review-32-06.png).
10. **The Google pages leave out printed dishes.** In 633 of 901 library films, at least one dish printed on the menu is missing from the film page, which still says "The menu comes from The Crown, Sandford". Hot Fuzz's page drops the roast chicken. Shaun of the Dead's drops the Full English and the Cornetto cones. The Witch's drops the baked eggs and the pottage.

## How it was tested

- Served the repo from /home/claude/movie-night-spinner (branch fix/review-31) on 127.0.0.1:8432. I used Playwright with Chrome, service workers blocked, the welcome screen skipped, and one browser at a time. TMDB images were blocked in the sandbox, so I did not mark down missing posters or stills.
- Desktop 1440x900: home, spin (landed on Dirty Work), menu zoom, prep sheet and shopping list, Save PDF dialog, candy bar, Galaxy Video (walk in, shelves, a tape back for Anchorman 2), the member card dialog in Galaxy, the guest pass panel from the cinema's Admit One stub, Match night, Quiz night, and a tab-key focus check.
- Phone 390x844 (scale 2, touch): 16 films opened (Hot Fuzz, Shaun of the Dead, Jurassic Park, Wall-E, Die Hard, The Godfather, Ratatouille, Gone with the Wind, The Castle, Train to Busan, Grease, Spirited Away, Gallipoli, Kung Fu Hustle, Paddington 2, Two Hands). I recorded which panel opens, the panel count, overflow and rendered text size. I also played the full daily quiz (ten questions, answers checked on screen each time), dealt a Match night deck, walked into Galaxy Video, and opened the Hot Fuzz film page.
- Menus: I rendered all 1,000 printed menus to text. I read 114 in full: 102 library menus across 33 layouts (pub, tavern, tearoom, banquet, home, dining car, saloon, hotel, campfire, canteen, kiosk, diner, cafe, club, dim sum, takeaway, truck, reception, newspaper, prom, motel, invite, lab, airline, bistro, stadium, galley, tiki, precinct, arcade, deli, bar tab, clubhouse), weighted to British, Australian, Asian, period and fantasy films, plus 12 hand-built ones. I then ran scripted checks over all 1,000 for em dashes, stray punctuation, money format, bar tab totals and short notes.
- Film pages: parsed all 1,000 /menu/ pages for titles, canonical links, JSON-LD validity and recipe times, and compared their dishes with the printed menus.

## Issues

### Quick fixes

1. **Where:** Crouching Tiger, Hidden Dragon (2000), drinks. **What:** "Warm osmanthus wine 1 taels". **Why it matters:** a grammar slip on a printed price looks machine-made. **Fix:** singular unit when the amount is 1 (taels, coins, crowns, silver).
2. **Where:** My Fair Lady (1964), American Pie (1999), Captain Marvel (2019), Cars 2 (2011), Peter Pan (1953). **What:** double full stops, for example "Carriages at midnight.." and "Stifler's mom..". **Why it matters:** obvious on paper. **Fix:** when joining wink lines, strip a trailing full stop before adding one.
3. **Where:** This Is Spinal Tap (1984), "HOW WE STACK IT" card. **What:** "8 SLICES DARK RYE BREAD" is listed twice. **Why it matters:** a recipe card with a repeated line looks careless. **Fix:** de-duplicate the stack list, or show the bread as the top and bottom layer once.
4. **Where:** Goldfinger (1964), scorecard total. **What:** prints "£1 11S"; the items add to £1 11s 4d. **Fix:** format pence properly in lower case and carry the pence into the total.
5. **Where:** Grease (1978), "ASK ABOUT PIE". **What:** "Frosty Palace burger, 4.50" while the menu says 1.50. **Fix:** take the board price from the same priced item.
6. **Where:** Kung Fu Panda 2 (2011) A4 and The Fifth Element (1997). **What:** a silken tofu dish has the note "Mr Ping's Secret Ingredient Soup, no peeking."; "Diva's opera night chilli garlic crab" is described as "wok-tossed with butter and parsley". **Fix:** match each note to its dish.
7. **Where:** Boy (2010), drinks. **What:** "Homebrew ginger beer 1.00" prints with no note. **Fix:** add a proper note, and add a build check that fails any printed item without one.
8. **Where:** cinema guest pass panel (Admit One stub) on desktop. **What:** "Everything works, saved on this phone only." and "on every phone you sign in on", shown on a computer. Galaxy's version correctly says "device". **Fix:** use "this device" in the cinema copy too.
9. **Where:** The Man from Snowy River (1982), Australia (2008), and other saloon menus. **What:** "REWARD £82" beside a "100 POUND REWARD" card; "REWARD £1942". **Fix:** use a period-sensible reward amount, not the film year or bill number.
10. **Where:** Boy (2010) header, Next Goal Wins (2023) section map, Priscilla (1994) tagline. **What:** "PROM NIGHT, CLASS OF 2010" for a 1984 hall dance; "CLUB LEVEL", "SUITES" at a village pitch; "Pie so good it ought to be a crime" at the Palace Hotel. **Fix:** let each film override these template strings, and blank them when they do not fit.
11. **Where:** Hot Fuzz (2007), Notting Hill (1999), Gladiator (2000). **What:** "Sandford is the safest village in England. Have a cuppa, Nick." signed Danny Butterman; "Well, you'll have to fight me for it. This is a very good brownie." signed William Thacker; two Maximus lines joined into one. **Fix:** swap in real lines, or reword as an unsigned house note.
12. **Where:** index.html. **What:** `lang="en"`, while the film pages declare en-AU, and the marquee h1 is split into letter spans that read as "REELDINNER". **Fix:** `lang="en-AU"` and an `aria-label="Reel Dinner"` on the h1.
13. **Where:** wip/tmnt-redesign/ii-door-hangers.html. **What:** contains an em dash and will be public if the wip folder is deployed. **Fix:** remove the dash, or keep wip out of the deploy.

### Medium

1. **Where:** bar tab layout, 17 of 35 menus (Deadpool, Star Wars, An American Werewolf in London, Top Gun: Maverick, Beerfest, Blade, El Mariachi, The Crow, Major League, Se7en, Sweet Home Alabama and others). **What:** "CARRIED OVER" and "RUNNING TAB" do not equal the sum of the prices above them. For example, Deadpool's items add to 106.25 but it prints 114.50. **Why it matters:** anyone who checks the tab sees it is fake, and it undercuts the "real menu" idea. **Fix:** compute both totals from the rendered prices at draw time, never from stored figures.
2. **Where:** library menus with bare prices (about half of those read: Braveheart, Gone with the Wind, Dune, Mulan, The Wizard of Oz, From Russia with Love, The Water Diviner, Casablanca, Danger Close, Hunt for the Wilderpeople, Crazy Rich Asians, Klaus and many more). **What:** no currency mark anywhere on the menu; banquet "per cover" totals like "89.50". **Why it matters:** fails "the right money for the place". **Fix:** give every film a currency (real or storybook) and print it, for example piastres for 1919 Istanbul, francs for Rick's, solari for Arrakeen, Galleons at Hogwarts.
3. **Where:** wrong money. **What:** Young Frankenstein in dollars, The Great Escape in cents, Oliver! "TWO CENTS", Hogwarts "£51.25", Snow White "35¢", Shrek and The Lion King in plain decimals. **Fix:** add a currency rule by setting (country, era, made-up world) and check every film against it.
4. **Where:** period pricing. **What:** The Prestige pie 7/9 (1890s), American Werewolf pint £3.00 (1981), Mad Max pie $4.25 (1979), The Dish scones $1.00 (1969), Red Dog 1970s prices under "EST. 2011". **Fix:** check the price era against the setting year, not the release year, and keep the header year consistent with it.
5. **Where:** price order. **What:** Crocodile Dundee crackling $5.75 above prawns; Lost in Translation wagyu sando ¥410 as the cheapest item; Two Hands potato scallops $8.25 above fish and chips $6.50; Deadpool pickled eggs $9.75; Psycho and Mad Max pickled onions above sandwiches and chips. **Fix:** a build check that cheap-bite dishes (chips, nuts, crackling, pickles, scallops) can never cost more than any real starter or main on the same card.
6. **Where:** phone, Ratatouille (2007) and the banquet layout (Gone with the Wind). **What:** Ratatouille opens on the whole page at about 5 to 6 px text with no panels (review-32-03.png); Gone with the Wind opens at about 7 px italic (review-32-04.png). **Why it matters:** fails "open on the food and be readable". **Fix:** give Ratatouille panels or a crop on the menu sheet, and raise the banquet bill of fare type or split it in two.
7. **Where:** phone, Grease (1978) and other three-page diner menus. **What:** opens on page 3, "The Menu, continued", not page 2 with the starters and burgers (review-32-05.png). **Fix:** open on the first page that has priced food.
8. **Where:** desktop, precinct layout (Dirty Work, 1998) and similar. **What:** the film title block "DIRTY WORK" is half covered by the sticky note when there is no logo image (review-32-07.png). **Fix:** move the note below the title block or give the fallback title its own clear space.
9. **Where:** film pages for library films. **What:** 633 of 901 pages leave out dishes printed on the menu (Hot Fuzz drops the roast chicken; Shaun of the Dead drops the Full English and the Cornetto cones). **Why it matters:** a visitor from Google sees a different menu from the one they print. **Fix:** list every printed dish on the page, marking extras without a recipe as "on the printed menu".
10. **Where:** Four Weddings and a Funeral (1994). **What:** "Welcome to Angus and Laura's Wedding" sits above "Glenthrist Castle, Carrie and Hamish request...", which mixes the first and third weddings. **Fix:** pick one wedding and keep every line to it.

### Large

1. **Where:** the printed notes on library layouts, across about 900 films. **What:** notes cut down to stubs or to film references with no dish ("Beef braised in ale.", "Wensleydale and cheddar.", "Pecorino, sheep's feta.", "Slow cooked potato.", "Rudi, who proposes to Fay in his new flat."), while the full note already exists in the phone text menu and on the film page. **Why it matters:** this is the owner's first rule ("never a bare name or a two-word scrap") and it shows up in most library menus. **Fix:** stop truncating. Write a print-length note for every library dish (dish first, then the film touch, about 8 to 16 words), and let each layout reflow or drop a decorative extra rather than cut the note. Add a build check that fails any printed note under six words or without a food word.
2. **Where:** the library content as a whole (money, period prices, price order, template text, anachronisms). **What:** the issues in Medium 1 to 5 and Quick fixes 9 and 10 come from shared rules, not one-off typos. **Why it matters:** the hand-built 97 are near 9.5, but the 900 library menus read closer to 6, and they are what most spins land on. **Fix:** run one pass per layout with a fixed checklist (currency printed and correct, setting-year prices, cheap bites below starters, totals computed, header strings fit the venue, ingredients fit the period, every quote verified or turned into a note). Re-render all 1,000 and run the scripted checks used in this review as a gate before release.
