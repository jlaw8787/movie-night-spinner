# Reel Dinner, review 42

I reviewed main at a52710aa, which is what is live, using only what the site prints: the photo-on dump of all 1,000 cards (every card face, print list, phone menu, prep sheet and shopping list), the 1,000 built film pages, and the site itself in Chrome. I did not open the handoff, ripple or earlier review files, audit/ops or git history. I wrote Python sweeps over every face, list, phone menu, prep sheet and film page for all 1,000 films: dashes, alcohol on kids-type cards, prices by layout and era, Australian words on American cards, American words that should stay Australian, notes that only repeat the dish name, dish name against note, film words repeated within a card, film page against card, and phone recipe steps with no time. Then I read 152 cards in full: 90 spread across all 134 layouts and every era, 30 kids, family, Christmas and wizard cards, and 32 hand-built one-offs. I opened 16 films in the browser at 1280 and 390 wide, including the printed menu and printed prep sheet (Close Encounters, The Godfather), the screen prep sheet, phone recipes and one film page. Every example below is quoted exactly as printed.

## Overall: 7.7 / 10

| Area | Score | One line |
|---|---|---|
| True to the film | 8.5 | Real venues, real lines, inspired one-offs; let down by dishes that contradict their own names and stock fillers that ignore the setting. |
| Dish notes | 7.5 | Mostly food first and tight (none over 19 words), but about 125 just repeat the dish name, about 70 contradict or leave out the named dish, and some one-offs lead with plot. |
| Prices | 8.5 | Money tracks the era well and homes, invites, galleys, receptions and banquets print none; a handful use the wrong money or price a place where nobody pays. |
| Template wording that suits the venue | 7.0 | Many layouts are cleverly reworded, but 40 banquet cards print "The Remove" and "Entremets" whatever the venue, and train timetables turn up on a bus and a cab. |
| Period-correct food | 8.0 | Strong regional and period detail, with a few clear slips (1607 Powhatan chicken with smoked paprika and lime soda). |
| Australian English and no em or en dashes (with the American card rule) | 6.5 | Zero dashes anywhere, but about a third of the cards I could place in the US print Australian food or everyday words on the card. |
| Desktop readability at 1280 | 8.0 | Clean and readable; the arcade layout is clipped on the left and Collateral shows a sliver of the next face. |

## What is strong

- No em or en dashes anywhere. My sweep of every face, list, phone menu, prep sheet, cue and every built page under menu/ found 0. The only spaced hyphen on any card is the deliberate "B I O - E X O R C I S T" on Beetlejuice.
- Kids, family, fairy tale, Christmas and wizard cards are alcohol-free on every surface. I swept 255 cards (the kids, fairytale, xmas and wizard themes plus anything with a Family or Animation genre, minus Die Hard and Love Actually) for 60 alcohol words. Every hit was a false alarm: ginger beer, root beer, cider vinegar, "cocktail franks", custard eggnog, "parsley liquor" on Oliver!. The swaps are clever: Ratatouille prints "Grape juice, 1947" for Ego's order, and The Muppet Christmas Carol has "God bless us mulled grape punch".
- The hand-built one-offs are the best thing on the site. Road to Perdition's Halfway Diner prints a coach ticket "from ROCK ISLAND to PERDITION", "Michael, eat your pie." and a "Wake coffee .10, Brown sugar, soft cream, Irish style. Strictly medicinal." Miller's Crossing has "One black hat, found in the woods. Claim it at the check room." Big Trouble in Little China prints a bilingual menu with "Pork-Chop Express ribs, Straight off Jack's truck." Kung Fu Panda has "Secret ingredient soup, Noodle soup, no secret ingredient."
- Regional food is often spot on. The Conjuring: "Rhode Island coffee cabinet", "Hide and clap cornmeal johnnycakes", "Rhode Island clam cakes". BASEketball: "Denslow Cup brandy old fashioned". Encanto: "Colombian hot chocolate, Hot dark chocolate with a cube of mozzarella melting in each mug." Hunt for the Wilderpeople: "Hot Milo, three heaped spoons, the crunchy bits floating on top."
- Gags that tie to the food: Heat's "Patty melt with grilled onions ... Ready in thirty seconds.", This Is Spinal Tap's "Dark rum and Coca-Cola over 11 ice cubes.", Demolition Man's "Three seashell crackers", Better Off Dead's "It's got raisins in it, Wobbly green lime jelly set with raisins, two recipes whose pages ran together."
- Prices follow the era. Taking the median printed price per card in dollars and cents: about 30¢ for films set before 1946, 50¢ for 1946 to 1965, $1.50 for 1966 to 1979, $2.50 for the 1980s, $3.85 for the 1990s, $4.95 for the 2000s and $6.95 from 2010. Euros, pounds, pence and coins appear where they belong (Leap Year in euros, The Prestige "Victoria sponge ... 2d", John Wick in gold coins).
- No-pay venues mostly print none: 0 of 63 home cards, 0 of 29 invites, 0 of 25 galleys, 0 of 12 receptions, 0 of 8 hospitals and 0 of 41 banquets (bar three single "one of everything" totals). The airline card for Catch Me If You Can prints "Included" instead of a price, which is a nice touch.
- Many templates are reworded to fit. BASEketball's stadium map reads "Front row, Back step, On the fence, Courtside" and "Bring your own cushion". Mad Max: Fury Road's place card is a "Water Ration Slip, One cup per person". Insidious turns the hotel layout into "At home, 2010".
- The phone menu is beautiful at 390 and puts the food first even where the card does not (Something About Mary's phone shows "Crumbed risotto balls, mozzarella middle, marinara." above the gag). Tabs, big type, one card per dish.
- The screen prep sheet is genuinely useful: step times, "Make ahead" lines, hands-on and longest-on-the-night totals, and a shopping list that adds up across dishes (The Godfather: breadcrumbs 80g plus 40g printed as "120g breadcrumbs").
- Film pages exist for all 1,000 films and 7,479 of 7,485 page dishes print the same name as the card.

## What holds it back

1. **American cards print Australian words, often inside the same dish line.** I placed 331 cards in the US with a sweep (two or more US place names on the card, or one plus dollar prices, and no pounds, euros or yen). At least 110 of those 331 print one or more Australian food or everyday words on the card: "crumbed" on 27, "mince" on 15, "Takeaway menu" on 12, "toasties" on 10, "beetroot" on 9, "prawns" on 12, "cos" on 8, "topside" on 7, "mum" on 6, "biscuit crust" or "biscuit base" on 7, plus "car park", "esky", "lift" and "full cream". The worst kind flips mid-line, so the name is American and the note is Australian. I found 11 shrimp to prawn flips on American cards and 8 fries to chips flips:
   - Catch Me If You Can: "Barry Allen shrimp cocktail, Poached prawns, Marie Rose, iceberg, lemon." (screenshot 04)
   - The Wolf of Wall Street: "Twisted Robin Hood king shrimp cocktail, Poached king prawns, Marie Rose, iceberg."
   - I Know What You Did Last Summer: "Billy Blue shrimp and grits, Garlic butter prawns over cheesy grits."
   - Kingpin: "Chilli cheese fries, Chips under beef chilli, cheddar and jalapeños."
   - Get Out: "Cold whole milk with a straw, Cold full cream milk with a straw."
   - Close Encounters: "Crispy potato bites with ketchup, Crisp potato bites with tomato sauce." (screenshot 03)
   - Kindergarten Cop (Astoria, Oregon): "Crumbed white fish fingers, oven chips, peas and tartare sauce." (screenshot 05)
   - The Lost World: Jurassic Park (San Diego): "Rare roast beef and rocket on white bread".
   - Honey, I Shrunk the Kids: "Ants on a log, Celery sticks with peanut butter and sultanas".
   - Serendipity: "Hotel lift club sandwich" and "double-fried chips".
   - Elvis: "the girls in the car park have been there since noon".
   - Lady Bird: "Mum's chicken, broccoli and rice bake". Collateral: "for Max's mum".
   - Three Men and a Baby prints both "Nappies are harder." and, on the same card, "Diapers, formula, powdered milk by the can."
   Australian food also lands on American cards: sausage rolls on 6 (Men in Black II "Truro post office sausage rolls", Wonder Woman 1984 "Food court sausage rolls"), meat pies (Revenge of the Nerds "A spare beef meat pie with tomato sauce", Four Christmases "Mini beef pies with puff pastry tops and tomato sauce") and "chicken salt" on Idiocracy and Screwed.

2. **Dish names and notes disagree, or the note leaves the named dish out.** My sweep flagged 351 dishes where the last word after "with" or "and" in the name never appears in the note; a hand check of a random 50 found 10 real faults, so roughly 70 site-wide. A second sweep for clashing pairs (sirloin and rump, shrimp and prawn, ketchup and tomato sauce) found 35, all checked. Examples:
   - Cliffhanger: "Gabe's ranger hut sirloin steak with foil-baked potatoes, Rosemary rump steaks grilled over coals". Kindergarten Cop has the reverse: "Rump steak and loaded spuds, Seared sirloin sliced over green salad". (screenshots 01, 05)
   - Nope: "Grilled tri-tip and beans, Rump cap grilled over coals and sliced". Tri-tip is not rump cap, so a home cook buys the wrong cut.
   - One Hundred and One Dalmatians: "Sooty puppy black sesame potato chips, Paper-thin crisps tossed in poppy seeds".
   - The Rock: "Sourdough with salted butter, Warm loaf, sliced thick, with oil and toasted sesame for dipping."
   - X-Men: First Class: "Hellfire Club pepper steak, Seared rib eye steaks with crisp roast potatoes." No pepper.
   - Pretty in Pink: "Blane's BMW turkey and avocado club" with no avocado in the note.
   - Oliver!: "Workhouse governors' roast chicken and bread sauce, Lemon and thyme roast chicken with roast potatoes and carrots."
   - To All the Boys I've Loved Before: "Korean yoghurt drink popsicles" is made from plain yoghurt, and the recipe lists "8 ice blocks of moulds and sticks".
   Worst of all, Cliffhanger's printed card drops a dish name completely: under "Campfire Treats" it prints only "Cinnamon apple and raisin filo strudel, served with cream." The name "Treasury cash apple strudel" shows on the phone and film page but not on the card. (screenshot 01)

3. **Phone recipes give oven and pan steps with no time.** The phone menu is where most people will cook, but the phone method strips the step times that the prep sheet has. I searched every phone recipe line that bakes, roasts, braises, simmers or grills and has no minutes, hours, "until" or doneness cue: 1,128 lines on 597 cards. A random 20 of those had 17 genuine faults, so about 950 steps on about 550 cards. Examples, as printed on the phone:
   - Hook: "Roast at 220°C with the potatoes in the tray." for a 1.8kg spatchcock (the prep sheet says 45 min). (screenshot 06)
   - The Addams Family: "Bake at 175°C." Hereditary: "Bake at 160°C." Road to Perdition: "Bake at 220°C."
   - Sister Act: "Braise covered at 150°C."
   - Back to the Future Part II: "Top the mini bases with a little sauce and cheese and bake for the reveal."
   - To All the Boys I've Loved Before: "Heat the oven to 180°C, roll into 16 balls and bake."
   Four phone recipes also cook the pasta twice, for example Home Alone 2: "Cook 180g dried macaroni in well salted water until just tender, then drain. ... Boil the macaroni and drain well." (also Spider-Man: Far From Home, How to Lose a Guy in 10 Days, Legally Blonde).

4. **Template wording that ignores the venue.** Counted by searching every face for each layout's fixed headings:
   - 40 of 41 banquet cards print "Second Course · The Remove" and "Third Course · Entremets", Victorian service terms, at Mad Max: Fury Road's Citadel, Violent Night, Hot Shots! Part Deux, Mars Attacks!, Enter the Dragon, Guardians of the Galaxy Vol. 2 and The Wolverine. Mad Max prints "Fourth Course · The Cordials" over a cucumber cooler. (screenshot 11)
   - The dining car's "Dinner Timetable, 1st call Starters 5.30 PM" and "Write your order on the check. Prices subject to change." prints on 10 cards, including Speed's hijacked city bus ("Bus 2525"), Collateral's cab ("CAB 5 ... Ida's hospital chicken noodle soup $10.50") and Romancing the Stone's "Cartagena Bus Cantina". (screenshot 10)
   - The school canteen prints "Period 1, Homeroom, Period 2, Math" at Police Academy.
   - Rain Man's hospital cafeteria, "Dr Bruner's patients · Meal order", serves a "Maple syrup bourbon smash" and a "Vegas gin martini".
   - "Now showing" prints twice in a row on 54 of 61 campfire faces in the dump (in print it shows as a poster badge and a stub, so this one is mild).
   - Something About Mary's card prints dot leaders that run to no price. (screenshot 09)

5. **Notes that restate the dish, lead with plot or carry filler tails.** Counted by sweep:
   - 149 notes add two or fewer words to the dish name; a hand check of 30 found 25 genuine, so about 125. "Wellness check crispy potato bites with ranch, Crisp crispy potato bites and buttermilk ranch." (Weapons; Mean Girls prints the same "Crisp crispy"). "Big badaboom lychee champagne, Champagne with lychee for opera night." (The Fifth Element).
   - 44 notes on 43 cards end with "no alcohol" on drinks that never had any, mostly adult cards: Close Encounters "Fresh strawberry and lemon juice lemonade over ice, no alcohol.", Galaxy Quest "Blue lagoon mocktail ... no alcohol."
   - Some one-offs lead with the plot. There's Something About Mary prints the gag first on every dish: "Puffy's body-cast chicken cordon bleu, Puffy ends up in a full body cast.", "Hair gel meringues with lemon curd, Meringues. Mary thinks it's hair gel." (screenshot 09). Others: Good Burger "Ed pelts a truck with ice cream. Choc-dipped vanilla cones.", Demolition Man "Fine dining fish tacos with pico de gallo, Taco Bell is fine dining now. White tablecloths for the taco course." (no food at all), Kung Fu Panda "Valley of Peace kung pao chicken, The busy village below the Jade Palace.", Three Men and a Baby "Shaken Vanilla Milk, Milk, vanilla, honey. Jack leaves disguised as a pregnant woman."
   - One film touch per card: 60 cards repeat the same rare film word in three or more dish lines. Close Encounters uses mashed potato in the fritter note, a main, the chicken note and the cue; Men in Black II uses "Ben's Famous" three times and worm guys three times.

6. **The printed prep sheet doubles every step number.** In print the steps run together as "1. 1. Lay the crackers out in rows of five. 2. 2. Top each row..." and labels jam: "SNACKPrep 5 min", "MAIN12 min to set first". Seen on both films I printed (Close Encounters and The Godfather), so it looks site-wide. The screen version is fine. (screenshot 08)

7. **Recipes that hide or garble ingredients.** The Godfather's arancini "You need" lists "400g cold cooked risotto, thick" and no arborio, butter, stock or parmesan, then step 1 says "soften 160g arborio in the butter, add 500ml hot stock ... stir in the parmesan". The shopping list has them; the recipe does not. (screenshot 07) Twelve phone recipes on 7 cards are a single paragraph with no ingredient list ("As written, serves 4", for example Hook's "Rufio's rainbow dips"). Hook's phone method also ends "Pile it all in the middle and eat with your hands. Bangarang." and "Bangarang" is already in the Rufio line, so the film word spills into method text.

8. **Food or money not true to the time and place.** From my 152 read cards plus targeted sweeps:
   - Pocahontas (1607): "Virginia harvest stew, Chicken thighs, pumpkin, corn, kidney beans and tomato with smoked paprika." and "Flit's blueberry mint cooler, Blueberries and fresh mint muddled with lime and soda water."
   - Captain America: The First Avenger (1943): "London pub ploughman's", a name from the 1950s and 60s.
   - Oliver! (1838): "brown sauce" and "golden syrup", both later.
   - Austin Powers: The Spy Who Shagged Me: "Austin's Pad, Swinging London, 1969" prices in American cents ("70¢", "$1.75"), and it is his own pad, where nobody pays.
   - Indiana Jones and the Temple of Doom, "Club Obi Wan, Shanghai's finest nightclub" (1935), and Kung Fu Hustle's Pig Sty Alley print American cents.
   - The Chronicles of Narnia: Prince Caspian prices a campfire at the Cair Paravel ruins in "3 silver pieces".
   - Yorkshire puddings appear on 18 cards, including American ones with no British link: Singin' in the Rain's studio canteen and Shutter Island's "Ashecliffe roast beef and Yorkshires".

9. **The Shining and Who Framed Roger Rabbit say "popsicle" where they mean a big ice cube.** "Bourbon, demerara syrup and bitters stirred over a big popsicle, orange peel." (The Shining) and "Fiery ginger beer over a big popsicle with lime and a drop of vanilla." (Who Framed Roger Rabbit). Both recipes underneath say "large ice block", so this looks like an automatic swap gone wrong. (screenshot 02)

10. **Film pages do not always print the card's words.** 59 page notes in 36 films differ from the card (my sweep looked for each page note inside the card text, ignoring case and punctuation). The Godfather page says "Ruby citrus syrup, cold seltzer. An orange on every table." where the card says "Ruby citrus syrup, cold seltzer." Casino's page says "Buttermilk batter, ten berries in every one." where the card says "Buttermilk batter. Count them. We did." My Cousin Vinny's page joins two lines with no full stop: "Butter & sharp cheddar The grits cross-examination." and "Cornmeal crust, remoulade Alabama's finest." (screenshot 12). Six page dish names are not on the card, including Cliffhanger's missing strudel.

11. **Desktop clipping.** At 1280 the arcade layout is wider than its window and shifted left, so the left part of the sheet is cut off (Toy Story, Wreck-It Ralph and Snatch show a half-empty header box with only "WRECK-IT RALPH, 2012" on the right). Collateral shows about 50px of the previous face at the left edge. (screenshot 10)

## To reach 9.5

1. Make one pass on every American card (by setting, not theme) that turns the card's Australian words into American ones and stops mid-line flips: shrimp in the name means shrimp in the note, fries means fries. Start with crumbed, mince, prawns, chips meaning fries, cos, beetroot, toastie, topside, biscuit crust, mum, car park, takeaway, lift, full cream, sultanas. Keep the recipes and shopping lists Australian.
2. Put a time on every cook step in the phone recipe, the same time the prep sheet already shows ("Roast at 220°C with the potatoes in the tray, 45 min."), and fix the four recipes that boil the pasta twice.
3. Run a name against note check on all 6,968 dishes and fix the 35 clashes plus the roughly 70 notes that drop the named part (sirloin and rump, tri-tip, sesame and poppy seed, pepper steak, bread sauce, avocado). Restore Cliffhanger's missing dish name.
4. Rewrite the roughly 125 notes that only repeat the dish name, cut the 44 "no alcohol" tails on adult drinks, and reorder the one-off notes so food comes first (There's Something About Mary, Good Burger, Demolition Man, Kung Fu Panda, Friday, Three Men and a Baby).
5. Give fixed template headings a venue check: drop "The Remove", "Entremets" and "The Cordials" from banquets that are not formal period dinners, keep the dining car timetable for trains only, and take "Homeroom" and "Math" off Police Academy.
6. Fix the printed prep sheet's doubled step numbers and run-together labels.
7. Fix the money and food slips: Austin's Pad with no prices (or shillings), Shanghai and Pig Sty Alley in local money, no prices at Cair Paravel, Pocahontas without chicken, paprika, lime and soda water, no ploughman's in 1943, no brown sauce or golden syrup in 1838.
8. Change "big popsicle" back to "big ice cube" on The Shining and Who Framed Roger Rabbit, and fix "8 ice blocks of moulds and sticks".
9. Make each film page note the card's exact words, and add the full stop between joined lines (My Cousin Vinny).
10. Fit the arcade layout inside its window at 1280 and stop Collateral's neighbour face showing.
11. List every ingredient in the "You need" list, including sub-recipes (Godfather arancini), and give the 12 "As written" recipes a proper ingredient list.
